# Role Journey Map

Date: June 11, 2026

RybexOS should guide each role from a clear starting point to a short operating action. The role journeys below describe the intended demo and product direction without adding role-specific routing yet.

## CEO / Executive

1. Start at `/command-center`.
2. Review leadership priorities.
3. Check cash at risk, blocked work, and stage/gate health.
4. Assign an owner or request a decision.
5. Drill into `/billing`, `/field-execution`, `/closeout`, or `/reports` only when an exception needs context.

Proof point: leadership can see what threatens margin, schedule, safety, acceptance, or cash without reading every record.

## Operations Leader

1. Start at `/command-center`.
2. Review decision queue and blocked gate movement.
3. Open `/projects` for D2 blockers or `/mobilization` for D3 field-start blockers.
4. Open `/field-execution` for D4 field signals.
5. Assign or resolve the next required action.

Proof point: operations can control work movement across D5O rather than chase status updates.

## Project Manager

1. Start at `/projects` or `/command-center`.
2. Review assigned actions, gate blockers, due dates, and required evidence.
3. Open `/changes` for recovery actions, `/billing` for pay app blockers, or `/closeout` for acceptance blockers.
4. Complete the record or route the decision.
5. Confirm the next gate/status movement.

Proof point: PMs see what must be completed to protect scope, margin, billing, and closeout.

## Superintendent

1. Start at `/mobilization` before work starts.
2. Confirm D3 readiness: JHA, locates, access, crew, equipment, materials, and work packages.
3. Open `/field-execution` during work.
4. Review today’s work packages, blockers, and field signals.
5. Escalate RFI/change/safety/quality issues when work cannot proceed cleanly.

Proof point: field readiness and field execution are connected instead of separated.

## Field Supervisor

1. Start at `/field-execution`.
2. Open `/field-execution/daily-report/new`.
3. Enter today’s work, quantities, safety/quality signals, blockers, and signoff.
4. Flag RFI or change prompts if the issue affects scope, cost, or schedule.
5. Review the outcome panel to confirm what evidence remains and what moves next.

Proof point: daily reports become proof and escalation, not just a diary.

## Finance/Admin

1. Start at `/billing`.
2. Review cash at risk, approved-not-billed changes, missing backup, retainage, and lien waiver issues.
3. Open a pay application or follow up aging payment.
4. Confirm required backup and owner.
5. Escalate commercial exposure where payment or recovery is at risk.

Proof point: finance can see what can be billed and what is blocking cash recovery.

## Safety Manager

1. Start at `/safety`.
2. Review overdue corrective actions, missing JHAs, and incident follow-ups.
3. Open `/safety/record/new` or `/safety/jha/new` as needed.
4. Assign or verify closure.
5. Confirm whether safety items affect field work or closeout.

Proof point: safety records are tied to work movement and acceptance readiness.

## Quality Manager

1. Start at `/quality`.
2. Review missing tests, failed inspections, open deficiencies, and punch items.
3. Open `/quality/inspection/new` or `/quality/deficiency/new`.
4. Verify correction and evidence.
5. Open `/closeout` to confirm acceptance-package readiness.

Proof point: quality evidence supports acceptance, closeout, and payment.

## Estimator

1. Start at `/pipeline`.
2. Review go/no-go decisions, bid deadlines, and pursuit risks.
3. Open `/pipeline/new` to structure a new opportunity.
4. Open `/reports` to review production-rate lessons and GC/vendor performance.
5. Feed future estimating assumptions and pursuit posture.

Proof point: D1 pursuit and Optimize learning form a loop.

## Admin

1. Start at `/admin`.
2. Review data mode, route health, persistence status, visual QA, and demo readiness.
3. Confirm seed/demo fallback and read-only pilot boundaries.
4. Run verification before demos.
5. Keep platform limitations transparent.

Proof point: the prototype can be shown safely without overstating production readiness.
